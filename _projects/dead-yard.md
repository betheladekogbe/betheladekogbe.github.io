*Dead Yard* is a four-channel experimental documentary installation created by **Rasheed Peters** as a living memorial to his father. Inspired by the Jamaican tradition of the dead yard—a communal gathering for mourning, remembrance, prayer, storytelling, and music—the project brings together documentary footage, family archives, sound, and memorial objects. The work explores how cultural rituals and grief are experienced when migration separates people from their communities.

## My contribution

I led the complete music-production process for the accompanying **Dead Yard EP**, from composing and producing the beats to recording all instrumental performances, arranging the songs, engineering the vocal sessions in my studio, and completing the final mixes and masters.

I collaborated with vocalists **Shelice Brown-Sanni** and **Savio (Justin Jimenez)** to reinterpret traditional Jamaican mourning songs, hymns, and nine-night music through contemporary arrangements and textures. The resulting recordings were released as an EP and formed an integral part of the installation's sonic experience.

## Music and installation

The musical score connects the work's spiritual, cultural, and familial themes. In Rasheed Peters's description of the installation, familiar hymns and mourning songs are reshaped through arrangements and textures to evoke the shifting experiences of memory, mourning, and home. The installation combines the music with environmental sounds such as birds, fire, voices, and silence.

## Exhibition

*Dead Yard* was presented on **June 9, 2023**, as part of **Northwestern University's Documentary Media MFA Showcase**. It appeared in *Binding Domains*, a showcase presentation alongside Laura Gede's *Passive Transport*. Marquise Mays's contemporary review also describes the installation in Northwestern University's Abbot Hall.
