Producing a live service is only the beginning of the media process. The next challenge is translating one recording into material suited to different audiences and distribution channels.

At Pentecostal Tabernacle, I coordinated recurring editing assignments, set deadlines, provided examples, reviewed work, and helped manage deliverables ranging from **full-length program videos and sermon excerpts** to audio episodes, event photography, and vertical social clips.

## Building a repeatable editorial pipeline

The goal was to avoid relying on an editor to guess the final use of a recording. A useful production brief should answer four questions: What material are we starting with? What needs to be delivered? Who is it for? When does it need to be ready?

## Scheduling work and guiding other editors

The structure extended to sermon video editing. In March 2026, for example, my assignment brief specified that an editor should prepare the complete service video, extract the sermon as separate audio and video, and deliver at least one 15–90 second short-form clip. Dates, reference examples, and instructions were included directly in the task.

Other assignments involved editing event photography and syncing externally recorded audio to a filmed service when the camera's audio was unreliable. I also outlined ways to present behind-the-scenes footage together with the main program feed and production communications.

These tasks combine practical technical knowledge with **team management**: translating a creative request into a clear assignment, organizing handoffs, and giving others the information they need to execute well.

## Creative supervision, not credit-taking

My role in many of these assignments was **briefing, supervising, and coordinating** other editors—not personally editing every deliverable. That distinction matters to me.

A strong media team shouldn't depend on one person doing every technical task. It needs consistent standards and a process where different contributors can deliver work with confidence.

## What I took forward

This experience strengthened how I approach multimedia production as a system rather than a series of disconnected files. The same principles apply in educational media: clear learning objectives, reusable templates, manageable deadlines, review checkpoints, and media packaged for the audience and platform.
