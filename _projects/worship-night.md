**Worship Night: Hallelujah** was a live worship event produced at Pentecostal Tabernacle in Cambridge on **November 8, 2025**. The event brought together music, stage presentation, and a coordinated technical production to serve both the in-person audience and people watching online.

My responsibilities included **directing the video production, switching the live program, and coordinating a 12-person production team**. The project brought together camera work, audio/video coordination, photography, and livestreaming in a single high-pressure event.

## Preparing a full evening of live content

Worship Night required production teams to coordinate around rehearsals, a detailed music program, performance cues, and changing stage activity. The week before the event included a dress rehearsal intended to run the whole evening from start to finish.

For me, the challenge was translating that preparation into decisions made in real time: choosing shots, communicating across the production team, following the musical flow, and keeping the stream cohesive for people who were not in the room.

## A second opportunity: showing the direction process

In August 2026 I also assigned a follow-up editorial concept using **the program video, a behind-the-scenes camera, and the production communications recording**. The brief asked for synchronized horizontal and vertical edits, a picture-in-picture view of the crew's work, and several short reels highlighting interesting production moments.

That brief captures what makes this event valuable in a professional portfolio. The finished livestream shows the audience experience; a synchronized behind-the-scenes edit would help explain the creative and technical decisions made by the team. The brief is documented, but I am not representing the proposed behind-the-scenes edits as finished or published.

## My role and takeaway

This production demonstrates leading people in a live environment: making decisions while multiple departments are active, communicating clearly, and protecting the audience experience throughout a long performance.

It also reflects the production philosophy I continue to use: support skilled operators with a strong plan, then give them the direction and trust they need to execute it.
